# E35: Frozen function duels of genuine mod97 grokking candidates

> Status: both the near tier and the far tier completed (3 cases each × 2 subsets × 16 initializations = 192 training runs). Evidence level: dynamic-preference evidence across trained networks, single reference MLP protocol.

## 1. Question

Before grokking completes — when the network already fits the training set 100% but has not yet generalized — the complete function at that point (denoted **B**), and the final rule obtained after grokking completes (denoted **A**), are made to compete again inside a **brand-new unified small network**: which one is preferred? This directly tests "the later-arriving rule is preferred in function space", without relying on the original training trajectory.

The candidates come from the mod97 experiment of the original Power et al. Transformer (two layers, width 128, 70% data, CE + 0.1 amplitude-variance regularization): A is the final mod97 division mapping; B is the last actual complete mapping with "training all-correct, test ≤90%" before that same trajectory completed (verified one by one against the hashes of the originally archived functions, not a newly fabricated rule that "looks more complex"). The far-tier B is taken as the one with the highest raw answer CE among the pre-completion "training all-correct, test ≤50%" candidates.

## 2. Protocol

- Duel training set: the 6,518 inputs on which A/B outputs agree; the conflict set (where A/B disagree) is only used to measure preference, with no external ground truth assigned;
- Reference network: `194→128→128→239` GELU+LayerNorm MLP (72,815 parameters); Adam (lr=0.001, wd=0), fixed 50,000 steps; two independent training subsets per tier × 16 initializations;
- Each B must first be fitted to all-correct (CE≤0.01) by a network of the same architecture before it is allowed into a duel — passing this representational-capacity pre-check rules out "B simply cannot be learned at all".

## 3. Results

| Tier | Source CE (multiple relative to completion) | Conflict points matching A | Conflict points matching B | Complete implementation of A | Complete implementation of B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Near 0/1/2 (B deviates from A at 365/337/289 points) | 0.0004–0.002 (5–22×) | 99.89–99.95% | 0% | 15/96 | 0/96 |
| Far 3/4/5 (B deviates from A at 2,713–2,731 points) | 0.11–0.12 (1153–1630×) | ≈99.91% | ≈0% | 17/96 | 0/96 |

- **All 192 models fully fit the duel training set, and on the conflict points they side with A almost unanimously**; the fraction recovering A in the complete 9,312-point mapping is 15/96 in the near tier and 17/96 in the far tier — **no model ever ends up implementing B**;
- The remaining functions are not arbitrarily scattered wrong mappings, but functions very close to A (differing from A on average at only about 2.3/9,312 inputs);
- Paired near-vs-far comparison: complete-A recoveries 15/96 → 17/96, a net gain of 2 (the interval contains 0) — **a larger source-loss gap does not raise the exact-recovery rate**;
- After fitting, the models keep concentrating toward the vicinity of A: the first complete A appears markedly later than the moment of hard fitting of the training set.

## 4. Relationship to the "many-competitor" picture of grokking

This round of results is consistent with that picture and can serve as its dynamic counterpart: the original trajectory's early candidate B (closer to a training-set-specific complex extension) completely loses function population in the new learning system, and the networks instead concentrate on A and its immediate neighbors. It supports the dynamic fact that "the post-completion rule is preferred"; one testable but not directly measured explanation is that the pre-grokking competing cluster contains many functions whose contraction coefficients are only slightly above the target rule's, producing slow screening and a late takeover. Testing this explanation requires a direct measurement of the loss-resolved contraction spectrum of the competing cluster; this round does not treat it as established.

## 5. Limits (must be kept together with the results)

1. This is **dynamic-preference evidence across trained networks**: the candidates come from the original Transformer trajectory, and the duels take place in a separate unified small MLP; it is not a static-volume or exact-K measurement of the original architecture, nor does it establish an architecture-independent K ordering;
2. "Conflict points match A ≈99.9%" does not equal "≈99.9% of the models completely implement A" — the complete-A recovery rate is only about 16%;
3. The K difference between the near-tier source and A was not directly measured; the loss gap is only the basis for choosing the stages;
4. Single task (mod97), single reference network, 192 models; the fitting pre-check for the first-round far tiers 4/5 was not done (far 3 already passed).

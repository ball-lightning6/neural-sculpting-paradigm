## Experiment overview {#overview}

## Scientific question

Test the strong claim:

$$
P_{\mathrm{trained}}(f\mid D)
\stackrel{?}{=}
P_{\mathrm{init}}(f\mid f\models D).
$$

This experiment does not deny the simplicity bias of the initialization function prior; instead, it judges whether real optimization merely deletes initialization functions that do not satisfy the training labels and then samples from the remaining functions in proportion to their original mass.

## Expected reproduction results

Experiment 1a:

- At first fit, the TV between the trained distribution and the hard-conditioned prior is about 0.10--0.31;
- after post-fit 100 steps it is about 0.54--0.64, with a sampling-noise baseline of about 0.01--0.04;
- in `rule30_k3` the dominant function undergoes a rank reversal;
- with the same initialization and the same final training set but different training histories, about 19.5%--28.2% of the complete functions still differ after post-final 2,000 steps.

Experiment 1b:

- In the initialization prior, constant 0/1 account for about 14.01%/13.72% respectively, and non-constant functions about 72.27%;
- the single-sample hard-conditioned prior assigns the target constant about 27.5%--28.0%;
- under real training, all four groups reach 128/128 models becoming the target constant after one step;
- compatible non-constant seeds that already satisfied the training point at initialization also all migrate, 45/45 and 47/47;
- the trained distribution versus the static conditional prior has TV about 0.72, JS about 0.52 bit, and hard-function entropy about `5 bit -> 0 bit`.

## Boundary of the theoretical verdict

What may be claimed:

> Under these explicit protocols, the optimizer systematically transports the mass of functions that already satisfy the training set; the post-training function distribution is not a one-shot passive conditioning of the initialization hard-function prior.

What may not be claimed from this:

- that the initialization prior is unimportant;
- that every Bayesian account is wrong;
- that all optimizers, networks, and tasks exhibit the same collapse;
- that different paths necessarily never merge after infinite training time;
- that hard-function stability means logits or internal representations stop changing.

## Why this experiment was run, and how it was judged {#motivation}

## 1. Why it was done

In more than a hundred early synthetic tasks, the training data covered only a tiny fraction of a huge input space, yet the networks often converged stably across seeds to the generating rule. [Dingle et al. (2018)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-03101-6), [Valle-Pérez et al. (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08522), and [Mingard et al. (2021)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v22/20-676.html) provide the closest explanation: the network architecture already induces a non-uniform function prior at initialization, and training mainly deletes the functions that do not satisfy the labels, so that

$$
P_{\mathrm{trained}}(f\mid D)
\approx
\frac{P_0(f)\mathbf1[f\models D]}
{\sum_gP_0(g)\mathbf1[g\models D]}.
$$

Being severely under-constrained yet generalizing stably forces this picture to carry a very strong quantitative prediction: the **total conditional prior mass** of all candidate functions that deviate from the generating rule but still satisfy the finite training set must be much smaller than that of the target rule. Although counterintuitive, this requirement cannot be ruled out by intuition alone, because the exponentially small probability of a single function could be cancelled by an exponentially large number of candidates. It must be measured directly in an enumerable space.

## 2. Competing hypotheses

### H-static: hard conditioning

1. The trained distribution should be close to the hard-conditioned initialization prior within finite sampling error;
2. once the training set no longer changes, post-fit age should not systematically change hard-function odds;
3. the static posterior depends only on the final training set, so the order in which samples were added should leave no persistent differences;
4. initialization functions that already satisfy the training labels need not undergo any further systematic migration.

### H-transport: optimization-induced transport

1. The trained function distribution can deviate significantly beyond the sampling noise of the conditional prior;
2. continued post-fit optimization can still change the odds of specific functions;
3. different training histories with the same final training set can produce different functions;
4. even when the initialization function already satisfies all training labels, optimization can still transport it to another compatible function.

H-transport does not predict that migration necessarily goes toward the human-defined lowest-complexity function, nor does it deny that the initialization prior provides a zeroth-order bias.

## 3. E01a adjudication design

- Complete function space: `3-bit -> 1-bit`, 256 hard functions in total;
- network: `3 -> 64 x 10 -> 1 tanh`, `sigma_w=1`, `sigma_b=0.2`;
- prior: 1,048,576 initialization networks;
- training: 8,192 initializations per condition, full-batch Adam, learning rate `1e-3`;
- conditions: `000->0/1` and the `k=2/3/4` nested samples of Rule30;
- time: first fit, post-fit 100/1,000 steps, margin 2/4/8;
- paths: direct, forward, reverse, each with 4,096 paired initializations;
- null distribution: repeated sampling from the same static posterior, bootstrapped 1,000 times.

Primary criterion: the per-function TV/JS must significantly exceed the sampling fluctuation produced by the prior and the training sample size. Complexity histograms, average accuracy, or LZ are not used as core criteria.

## 4. E01b adjudication design

- Network: `3 -> 1024 x 3 -> 1`, GELU+LayerNorm, Adam, no weight decay;
- prior: 4,096 initialization networks;
- conditions: four single-sample cases `000/111 -> 0/1`;
- 128 paired initializations per condition;
- snapshots: initialization, first fit, post-fit 1/2 steps;
- fair control: separately tally the seeds whose initialization already satisfies the training label but whose complete function is non-constant.

Primary criterion: if the target constant does not occupy an overwhelming mass in the hard-conditioned prior, yet training still collapses all seeds onto the target constant, and the prior-compatible non-constant seeds also all migrate, then the hard-conditioning sampling model is directly rejected.

## 5. Boundaries explicitly reserved before seeing results

- E01a actually uses Adam, so the results cannot be automatically generalized to all SGD;
- identical hard functions do not imply identical logits, margins, and representations;
- finite-time path dependence does not imply that paths never merge in infinite time;
- the results can only refute the strong version of passive filtering by the hard-function prior; they do not refute all continuous Bayesian or optimizer-dynamics-containing probabilistic descriptions.

## Results, interpretation, and limits {#results}

## 1. E01a completeness

- Protocol version: `static_prior_vs_sgd_function_posterior_v1`;
- 1,048,576 prior samples;
- 5 conditions with 8,192 training trajectories each;
- three path-test protocols with 4,096 initializations each;
- all conditions reached hard exact on the training set;
- total runtime about 693.9 seconds;
- the raw function distributions, logits, bootstrap, path statistics, and plots are all in the ZIP.

### 1.1 Per-function TV far exceeds sampling noise

| Condition | First-fit TV | post-fit 100 TV | post-fit 1,000 TV | age 1,000 null p95 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `single_x0_y0` | 0.104 | 0.539 | 0.538 | 0.037 |
| `single_x0_y1` | 0.102 | 0.535 | 0.534 | 0.037 |
| `rule30_k2` | 0.309 | 0.638 | 0.640 | 0.028 |
| `rule30_k3` | 0.308 | 0.616 | 0.620 | 0.019 |
| `rule30_k4` | 0.309 | 0.602 | 0.607 | 0.017 |

All bootstrap tests reach the smallest `p≈0.001` resolvable with the current 1,000 repetitions. With the training set unchanged, the distribution continues to move away from the fixed static posterior within post-fit 100 steps.

### 1.2 Rank reversal of specific functions

`rule30_k3` at age 1,000:

| function ID | Static conditional prior | Adam trained distribution |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 254 | 43.61% | 16.53% |
| 238 | 17.32% | 79.32% |

This is not a simple sharpening of the original ordering by a drop in a common temperature, but a reordering of the specific continuation functions.

### 1.3 Training history is not fully washed out by 2,000 steps

After reaching the final training set and training for another 2,000 steps, the hard-function mismatch rates of same-initialization models are:

| Paired paths | Function mismatch rate | TV between the two empirical distributions |
|---|---:|---:|
| direct vs forward | 28.20% | 0.197 |
| direct vs reverse | 19.51% | 0.046 |
| forward vs reverse | 27.08% | 0.158 |

The stage sizes and total step counts of forward and reverse are matched, so these two give the cleanest order effect; direct sees the final training set from the very beginning, so the full difference between it and the incremental paths cannot be attributed solely to order.

## 2. E01b completeness and results

- Protocol version: `single_sample_prior_dynamics_1024_v1`;
- the prior covers 186/256 observed hard functions;
- four conditions with 128 models each, all fitting in one step;
- ages 0/1/2 all saved, with no transient reversal of the hard function.

Initialization prior:

| Type | Mass |
|---|---:|
| constant 0 | 14.01% |
| constant 1 | 13.72% |
| non-constant functions, total | 72.27% |

After single-sample conditioning, the static mass of the target constant is:

| Condition | Static target-constant mass | Post-training target-constant mass |
|---|---:|---:|
| `000 -> 0` | 27.49% | 100% |
| `000 -> 1` | 27.99% | 100% |
| `111 -> 0` | 27.76% | 100% |
| `111 -> 1` | 27.71% | 100% |

All four groups, 128/128, became the target constant after the first Adam update. Seeds that already satisfied the training point at initialization but were still non-constant functions also all migrated to the target constant, 45/45 and 47/47. If sampling independently from the static conditional prior, the probability that all 128 draws in a single group hit the target constant is about `10^-71`.

The final TV of the four conditions is about 0.720--0.725, JS about 0.515--0.521 bit; the static hard-function entropy is about 4.96--5.01 bit, and 0 after training. At age 2 the average training margin is about 11.3--11.5 and the average absolute logit on unseen inputs is about 10.8--11.1, so this is not an accidental flip near the threshold.

## 3. Stage verdict

The two experiments jointly reject:

> All simplicity bias is already fixed at initialization; training only deletes hard functions inconsistent with the labels and samples in proportion to the remaining prior.

The conclusion that is retained is:

> The initialization function prior is an important zeroth-order term, but the optimizer can systematically transport the mass of functions that already satisfy the training set, making the odds of specific functions depend on training age and history.

## 4. What may not be claimed

1. The simplicity bias of the initialization prior has not been denied;
2. continuous-logit, soft-likelihood, or explicit-dynamics Bayesian models have not been denied;
3. both E01a and E01b use Adam, so the results cannot yet be automatically generalized to arbitrary optimizers;
4. it has not been proven that the migration direction equals a decrease in machine-independent Kolmogorov complexity;
5. it has not been proven that paths still fail to merge after infinite time;
6. logits keep changing after the hard function stabilizes, so the two must not be conflated into the same object.

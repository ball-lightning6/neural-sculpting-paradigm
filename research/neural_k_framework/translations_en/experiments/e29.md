## Experiment overview {#overview}

## Purpose

Test whether, when input directions that are completely inactive during training
remain at the prior, the effective margin that grows as the loss decreases can
restore counterfactual invariance, and quantitatively predict no-decay Adam under
the same initialization distribution.

## Why this experiment was run, and how it was judged {#motivation}

## 1. Background

Izmailov et al. showed that the weights corresponding to input features that are
constantly 0 during training remain at the prior in the Bayesian posterior;
MAP / regularized SGD pushes these weights toward 0 and may therefore be more
stable under covariate shift.

E29 does not presuppose that a static method must automatically delete dead
weights. Instead it asks: even if the dead direction remains entirely at the
prior, is the effective margin brought by deeper loss sufficient to withstand
binary counterfactual flips?

## 2. Protocol

Training fully covers the 8 states of the three effective bits, but the dead bit
z is constantly 0. Testing changes z to 0.25, 0.5, 1, or 2 while the targets stay
unchanged. The function panel contains projection, XOR, majority, parity, and
MUX.

The comparison covers Gaussian constrained SMC, 512-seed no-decay Adam,
L2/MAP Adam, an MC-NNGP-like kernel, and direct integration of the standard
temperature-1 posterior.

## 3. Preregistered verdict

The primary comparison is the strict correct mass of no-decay Adam versus
matched-loss static SMC at z=1:

- the five-function mean absolute difference must be no higher than 0.10;
- the worst-function absolute difference must be no higher than 0.20.

L2 is a mechanism control and is not required to equal the static mass. z=2 is a
stronger amplitude extrapolation, used only to measure the boundary of
applicability; it does not participate in the primary pass criterion.

## Results, interpretation, and limits {#results}

## 1. Question and protocol

The inputs are (z,x0,x1,x2). The training set fully covers the 8 states of the
three effective bits, but the dead bit z is constantly 0; the counterfactual
tests change z to 0.25, 0.5, 1, and 2 in turn, while the target labels stay
unchanged.

The target functions are the five functions projection, XOR, majority, parity,
and MUX. The network is a 4->16->1 tanh network with 97 parameters in total. The
static branch uses Gaussian constrained SMC with 4 independent replicas of 2,048
particles each; the optimizer branch uses 512 paired initializations; the
NNGP-like baseline estimates the output covariance from 131,072 prior networks.

The primary preregistered comparison is the strict correct mass of Adam without
weight decay versus matched-loss static SMC at z=1. A mean absolute difference no
higher than 0.10 and a worst function no higher than 0.20 counts as a pass.

## 2. Main result

The original package's automatic summary reports:

- mean absolute difference: 0.001001;
- maximum absolute difference: 0.004150;
- primary_static_prediction_pass=true.

The automatic summary uses an approximate match between the Adam endpoint and the
deepest SMC layer; the two losses still differ by a factor of about 20--60. After
re-matching strictly using the Adam trajectories saved in the package, the
log-loss distance of all five functions does not exceed 0.057, and the z=1
conclusion is unchanged:

| Function | SMC z=1 strict mass | Adam z=1 strict mass | Absolute difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| projection | 1.000000 | 1.000000 | 0 |
| XOR | 1.000000 | 1.000000 | 0 |
| majority | 0.999878 | 1.000000 | 0.000122 |
| parity | 0.999268 | 1.000000 | 0.000732 |
| MUX | 0.995850 | 1.000000 | 0.004150 |

Therefore, on the frozen primary criterion, the static low-loss mass predicts the
dead-bit counterfactual behavior of real no-decay Adam almost exactly.

## 3. It is not that the dead weights were secretly compressed to zero

The dead-column variance in the deepest SMC layer is 0.975--1.077, and the mean
squared norm of the 16-dimensional dead column is 15.68--17.41, consistent with
the theoretical value 16 under the N(0,I) prior. In no-decay Adam, the maximum
absolute change of the dead column is strictly 0, with variance about 1 and
squared norm about 16.

Although the dead weights remain at the prior throughout, the SMC z=1 strict
correct mass increases systematically as the loss deepens:

- at epsilon of about 0.2, each function is about 0.53--0.90;
- at epsilon of about 0.02, each function is about 0.95--1.00;
- at epsilon of about 0.001, each function is about 0.996--1.00.

The mechanism is not identifying and deleting the dead bit; rather, the effective
branch obtains a larger margin at lower loss, so that dead perturbations of prior
scale are insufficient to change the hard function.

## 4. L2/MAP mechanism control

Explicit L2 compresses both the dead-column variance and the squared norm of Adam
to near 0; the z=1 strict masses are:

- projection, XOR, majority, MUX: 1.000;
- parity: 0.998.

The static SMC matched to its higher endpoint loss achieves only 0.934, 0.955,
0.843, and 0.794 on XOR, majority, parity, and MUX respectively. This difference
reproduces the mechanism of Izmailov et al.: MAP-style regularization actively
selects dead weights of 0, whereas BMA / static mass retains their prior
randomness.

## 5. Stronger shift exposes the boundary

At z=2 with strictly matched loss:

| Function | SMC strict mass | No-decay Adam strict mass |
|---|---:|---:|
| projection | 0.9827 | 0.9824 |
| XOR | 0.7361 | 0.8828 |
| majority | 0.8984 | 0.9316 |
| parity | 0.6472 | 0.8281 |
| MUX | 0.7456 | 0.9062 |

So the near-exact agreement between the static and optimizer branches has a clear
scope: the standard binary flip z=1 passes, but after extrapolating the unseen
input amplitude to 2, the complex functions show an optimizer reweighting
advantage of about 0.15--0.18. This is not a failure of the primary criterion,
but it limits the extrapolation of the conclusion.

## 6. NNGP result

For small ridges able to fit the z=0 training branch, the NNGP is strictly
correct on the z=1 branch for all five functions. This experiment therefore does
not establish that the NNGP cannot handle the dead bit. At z=2, high-frequency
functions such as XOR and parity begin to fail, showing that it likewise has a
shift-scale boundary.

## 7. Sampling diagnostics and conclusion

The pCN acceptance rate in the deepest SMC layer is 0.298--0.302, and the z=1
posterior-predictive disagreement across the four replicas is 0; the run took
410.18 seconds, with peak GPU memory of about 65 MB.

Claims that can be made:

1. the loss-resolved static volume accurately predicts no-decay Adam on the
   binary dead-bit counterfactual;
2. deep loss restores invariance through margin rather than through posterior
   contraction in the dead direction;
3. explicit L2/MAP obtains stronger robustness through a different mechanism;
4. the NNGP also handles this round's z=1 task, so the finite-width method does
   not establish an exclusive advantage over the NNGP;
5. stronger shift re-amplifies the static-versus-optimizer difference.

It cannot be claimed that this Boolean result overturns the BMA failure under
real MNIST/CIFAR covariate shift, nor that the static volume equals SGD for
arbitrary dead features or arbitrary perturbation amplitudes.

## 8. Standard temperature-1 HMC target distribution

To answer how the standard Bayesian HMC used by Izmailov et al. behaves on this
task, we additionally drew 1,048,576 parameter samples from the Gaussian prior
and reweighted them by the exact Bernoulli likelihood exp(-8*mean_BCE). This
importance integral has exactly the same target distribution as temperature-1
HMC; the ESS of each function remains 62%--93% of the total samples, with no
deep-tail degeneracy.

| Function | ESS ratio | Posterior mean loss | z=0 BMA accuracy | z=1 BMA accuracy | z=0 strict sample mass | z=1 strict sample mass |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| projection | 0.621 | 0.6706 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.0473 | 0.0362 |
| XOR | 0.924 | 0.7234 | 0.750 | 0.750 | 0.000004 | 0.000005 |
| majority | 0.737 | 0.6920 | 0.625 | 0.625 | 0.00508 | 0.00455 |
| parity | 0.932 | 0.7245 | 0.500 | 0.500 | 0 | 0 |
| MUX | 0.779 | 0.6998 | 0.750 | 0.750 | 0.000171 | 0.000141 |

Therefore standard temperature-1 HMC fails on this 8-sample, 97-parameter scaled
task, and for the four complex functions it does not even fit the BMA on the
training branch adequately. The failure is not that the sampler failed to find
the posterior, but that the posterior itself does not contract into the deep-loss
region. Continuing to run HMC at the same temperature will not solve this; only
duplicating the data, lowering the temperature, or explicitly entering a lower
epsilon pushes the distribution toward the successful static layers described
above.

This result does not contradict the original paper. The original paper's
large-data models already have high in-distribution accuracy and mainly expose
the OOD dead-pixel degradation; this scaled task has too little data, so the
under-contraction of the temperature-1 posterior appears on ID first. It instead
clearly demonstrates the extra information that the loss-resolved profile carries
relative to a single-temperature posterior.

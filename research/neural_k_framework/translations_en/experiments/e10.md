## Experiment overview {#overview}

## Purpose

E10 first uses a multi-task, large-scale static scan to test whether "lower loss universally increases the mass of simple functions", and then, targeting the problems of training-set ambiguity and external complexity proxies, constructs simple/complex function pairs whose training outputs are completely identical but whose representational difficulty is strictly ordered, for a direct odds test.

## Why this experiment was run, and how it was judged {#motivation}

## 1. Why the broad scan failed first

In the first stage, projection, majority, parity, and random balanced training constraints were combined in the full 4-bit space, scanning `k=2,4,6,8` and two network measures:

- `tanh 16x2`: 67,108,864 prior samples;
- `GELU+LayerNorm 1024x3`: 100,663,296 prior samples.

However, a "human-specified generating rule" is not necessarily the simple continuation preferred by the network under the current finite training set; ANF, DNF, decision trees, and the composite rank do not necessarily correspond to the network's language either. Under severely underconstrained conditions, a shift of low loss toward another threshold function cannot be directly counted as a simplicity failure.

## 2. Two cleaner discrimination principles

1. Use obviously simple rules that the samples are sufficient to expose, such as projection, AND, and OR;
2. Directly compare a pair of functions, where complex is obtained from simple by adding discrete exceptions, and:
   - the two are pointwise completely identical on the training set;
   - they have the same full truth table Hamming weight;
   - simple is a single-layer linear threshold function;
   - complex is proven non-linearly-separable by linear programming;
   - the ANF, normal form, and decision tree proxies of complex are all strictly larger.

Training samples are drawn only from the agreement set of the two functions, using `k=10,12,14`.

## 3. Primary decision quantity

For the simple function `f_s` and the complex function `f_c`, define:

$$
R(q)=
\frac{P_q(f_s)/P_q(f_c)}{P_{hard}(f_s)/P_{hard}(f_c)},
$$

where `q` is the lowest raw BCE quantile and `P_hard` is the baseline that only satisfies the same hard constraints. `R(q)>1` means that lower raw BCE further increases the odds of the simple rule.

raw BCE is the sole primary endpoint, because it is the loss that the corresponding training actually optimizes. normalized BCE and fixed-scale serve only as mechanism diagnostics and do not participate in judging the truth of the main proposition.

## 4. Final reliability criterion

The old automatic summary mistakenly only required the actual total of simple+complex in the tail to be sufficient. Because the baseline may already be extremely biased toward simple, 0/1 hits of complex would dominate the odds. The final rule was changed to:

$$
E_0[N_s]=N_s^{base}q\ge20,
\qquad
E_0[N_c]=N_c^{base}q\ge20.
$$

For each condition, the deepest quantile satisfying this rule is selected, and a Fisher exact test is used to compare the simple/complex probabilities of "entering the low-loss tail". This criterion was triggered by a statistical reliability concern after the results were returned but before the directions were read, and must be recorded separately from the old ZIP summary.

## 5. Boundaries

- This is still a static reference measure, not the SGD endpoint distribution;
- the 12 reliable comparisons are concentrated in the moderate variants of projection, AND, and OR;
- high variants and parity are often not adjudicable because of too few prior hits;
- the 12/12 result demonstrates a strong tendency within the tested function pairs, not a theorem for all loss ranges, function pairs, and architectures.

## Results, interpretation, and limits {#results}

## 1. The broad complexity-proxy scan refutes the strong version

The first stage analyzed 24 constraint groups for each of the two networks. After removing conditions without a reliable low-loss slice, according to the composite complexity panel at that time, the proportions of "becoming simpler" were approximately:

| Measure | Valid conditions | Composite complexity decrease | Mass increase in simplest 10% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| raw BCE | 44 | 56.8% | 34.1% |
| normalized BCE | 44 | 29.5% | 15.9% |
| fixed scale | 42 | 33.3% | 21.4% |

This refutes the strong version that "under any finite constraint, lower loss monotonically simplifies according to external Boolean complexity proxies". A large number of so-called failures actually shifted toward linear threshold functions, showing that training-set ambiguity and the mismatch of complexity language are severe.

## 2. The prior baseline is already strongly biased toward simple rules

In the GELU-LN `1024x3` hard-exact baseline:

| Rule pair | simple count | complex count | Ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `x0` | 226,375 | 1,794 | 126:1 |
| `x0 AND x1` | 38,404 | 2,984 | 12.9:1 |
| `x0 OR x1` | 137,064 | 482 | 284:1 |

The high-complexity four-exception variants are mostly too rare to reliably enter the pairwise test.

## 3. Final re-audit: 12/12 for raw BCE

Using the final criterion that the expected hits of both simple and complex under the null hypothesis are no fewer than 20, raw BCE yields 12 reliable comparisons:

- 12/12 show increased simple odds;
- 12/12 are significantly biased toward simple by the Fisher two-sided test;
- the GELU nine groups average `log2 odds enrichment=3.40`, about 10.6x;
- the tanh three groups average `log2 odds enrichment=2.34`, about 5.1x.

Representative results:

| Architecture/rule | k | retained fraction | odds enrichment | Fisher p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| GELU `x0` | 10 | 2% | 13.59x | `5.88e-8` |
| GELU `x0` | 12 | 2% | 26.71x | `2.00e-16` |
| GELU `x0` | 14 | 2% | 81.99x | `1.96e-18` |
| GELU AND | 12 | 1% | 9.96x | `3.95e-14` |
| GELU OR | 10 | 5% | 8.49x | `4.85e-10` |
| tanh AND | 12 | 20% | 6.00x | `1.58e-10` |

Under the normalized measure, 9/12 have a positive direction; under fixed-scale, only the nine GELU groups satisfy the final expected counts, of which 6/9 have a positive direction. They are mechanism diagnostics and do not participate in the raw primary adjudication.

## 4. Theoretical role

E10 directly supports, on the restricted function pairs:

> For function pairs that satisfy completely identical training hard constraints but are strictly ordered in the network's representation language, entering a lower true raw BCE sublevel set significantly increases the relative odds of the simple function.

This result separates "complex functions also have equally low-loss solutions" from "complex functions hold equally low-loss probability mass". It does not add a complexity objective to training; the network still only optimizes raw BCE, and the simplicity enrichment is an empirical consequence of the static geometry.

## 5. Boundaries and subsequent corrections

- The result does not grant prior privilege to the researcher-generated scripts; the simple/complex ordering is constructed from independent function pairs;
- the failure of the broad scan remains valid, showing that external complexity proxies cannot replace architecture-relative difficulty;
- a static odds tendency does not equal the optimizer distribution; E07 has directly shown that the numerical values can deviate significantly;
- 12/12 only tests the terminal reliable slice selected for each group; it does not prove that the odds are monotonic along the entire loss path;
- later AND shortcut and deep-tail crossover experiments will further show that the dominant function and the volume ordering can swap order with the loss scale; therefore E10 is strong positive evidence, not an unconditional law.

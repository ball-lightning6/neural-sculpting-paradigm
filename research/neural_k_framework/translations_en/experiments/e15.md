## Why this experiment was run, and how it was judged {#motivation}

## 1. Question

Is the multi-seed distribution before grokking a shared shortcut, or a shared rule skeleton plus seed-specific residuals? Looking only at validation accuracy or agreement cannot distinguish between these. Exact matching of complete functions may in turn be too strict: when each seed errs only on a different few points, the full hashes are still all different.

## 2. Setup

- Task: `x / y mod 97`, full input space 9,312;
- Nested training fractions 60%, 70%, 80%, 90%;
- 32 paired seeds per fraction, 128 models in total;
- MLP: `194 -> 512 x 3 -> 97` GELU+LayerNorm;
- Adam `1e-3`, no weight decay;
- Run for 121,101 steps;
- Compare function distributions at the same train CE first crossing.

## 3. Quantities reported simultaneously

- Full-target function mass;
- Full-function hash entropy and top repeated mass;
- Coordinate-wise modal function;
- Seed agreement;
- The final 10% holdout shared by all conditions;
- The relationship between train CE and unseen accuracy.

## 4. Boundaries

32 seeds can only resolve single-function mass no lower than 3.125%; the modal function is a coordinate-wise marginal combination and is not necessarily the complete function actually implemented by any single seed; this experiment measures Adam trajectories, not static volumes.

## Results, interpretation, and limits {#results}

## 1. Full-function mass requires sufficient data and sufficiently deep loss

| Training fraction | First target seed | Final target mass | Final function entropy |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 60% | Did not appear | 0/32 | 5.000 bit |
| 70% | Did not appear | 0/32 | 5.000 bit |
| 80% | step 78,000 | 1/32 | 5.000 bit |
| 90% | step 12,500 | 18/32=56.25% | 2.654 bit |

In the 90% condition, target mass rises step by step with matched train CE, from 3.125% at `3e-7` to 56.25% at `3e-11`. The non-target complete functions at 60%--80% differ for almost every seed, with no repeatable shared shortcut.

## 2. The modal function recovers far earlier than the full function of any single seed

| Training fraction | Modal function becomes the full target | First target seed |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 60% | Did not occur | Did not occur |
| 70% | Final modal accuracy 95.41% | Did not occur |
| 80% | step 18,000 | step 78,000 |
| 90% | step 2,000 | step 12,500 |

In the 90% condition, modal full accuracy was already 97.47% at step 500. The errors of different seeds are distributed around the target rule and cancel each other out in the coordinate-wise marginals, even though the full-function hashes are still all different.

## 3. The shared holdout rules out trivial gains from newly added training points

The final shared 10% holdout accuracies across training fractions are 2.29%, 16.19%, 51.83%, and 91.65%. 31/32 paired seeds are strictly non-decreasing across the four tiers; adjacent data-size comparisons also improve in almost all cases.

## 4. Stage verdict

E15 establishes:

1. Data size, loss depth, and optimization time are three distinct variables;
2. A wide complete-function distribution does not mean that the per-input marginals have not condensed around the target;
3. The more accurate description of the pre-grokking state is a shared target skeleton plus seed-specific residuals, rather than a shared complete shortcut;
4. Exact function probability, hash entropy, agreement, and the modal function must be reported side by side.

It does not prove static-volume causality, nor does it guarantee that the remaining 14 seeds in the 90% condition will necessarily reach the target eventually.

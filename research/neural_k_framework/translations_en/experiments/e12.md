## Experiment overview {#overview}

## Purpose

E12 tracks simple target functions across the full raw-BCE range and conducts a targeted investigation of the genuine counterexample that appeared in AND, n=10: why a structurally more complex compatible shortcut overtakes AND in deeper loss sections, and whether changing only the training-sample coverage can causally eliminate that advantage.

## Why this experiment was run, and how it was judged {#motivation}

## 1. Two strategies for avoiding circular reasoning

Earlier experiments exposed that "which function is simple" may depend on the training set and the network language. On August 20, two falsifiable strategies were frozen:

1. Choose obviously simple rules such as copy, NOT, AND, OR, and provide 10--14 training samples;
2. Construct pairs consisting of a simple function and its complex counterpart with explicit exceptions added, such that the two agree on the training set while the complex one is strictly harder at the representation level.

The first strategy was used for the full-loss sweep; the second was already tested by E10. E12 follows up on the AND counterexample that appeared under the first strategy.

## 2. Full-loss sweep

- Network: `4 -> 64 x 3 -> 1` GELU+LayerNorm;
- prior: 33,554,432 independent networks;
- Rules: copy, NOT, AND, OR, XOR;
- Training samples: `n=10,12,14`;
- Save the target-function mass in the full prior, the hard-exact cohort, cumulative tails, and non-overlapping loss bins.

XOR was hit only 17 times in the prior, below the preregistered threshold of 50 hits, so it does not enter the formal verdicts.

## 3. AND gap interventions

The original AND, n=10 contains all 4 positive examples and 6 negative examples, but omits several off-diagonal negatives, allowing shortcuts such as F500/F550 that rely on `x4`. The targeted static analysis fixes the architecture, sample count, and label ratio, changing only the positions of the negative examples:

- the original logical gap;
- closing each of the two shortcut branches separately;
- closing both sides simultaneously;
- the `x4=1` mirrored gap;
- the minimal balanced intervention: replacing only `1101 -> 1` with `1000 -> 0`.

The primary criteria are, within the hard-exact cohort as raw BCE decreases: function probabilities, odds, mean number of unseen exceptions, function entropy, and each function's signed margin on the training samples.

## 4. Interpretation boundaries

- The generator AND enjoys no privilege inside training; it is only an external evaluation benchmark;
- hard function ID still coarse-grains continuous margins and internal representations;
- static mass is not the SGD visitation probability;
- If fixed-function odds cross, then "the same simple function monotonically dominates in every loss region" fails outright; one cannot evade the counterexample by retroactively relabeling each winner as simpler.

## Results, interpretation, and limits {#results}

## 1. A general tendency and a genuine counterexample appear together

From the full prior down to the lowest 0.01% raw-loss tail, across the 12 decidable conditions of copy, NOT, AND, and OR, the target-function mass is strictly monotonically increasing in all of them. The enrichment of the lowest 0.01% relative to the prior is approximately:

- copy: 151--261x;
- NOT: 193--272x;
- AND: 87--269x;
- OR: 189--242x.

However, once the hard-exact cohort is fixed first and raw BCE is then lowered, AND, n=10 becomes a significant counterexample: AND falls from 8.75% to 6.81% in the lowest 10%, an enrichment of only 0.778x, with one-sided hypergeometric `p=8.60e-20`. Its rank drops from 1st to 9th at the lowest 0.1%.

## 2. Fixed-function odds genuinely cross

Under the original training set:

| lowest-loss fraction | AND | F550 | AND/F550 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100% | 8.75% | 6.02% | 1.45 |
| 50% | 7.99% | 7.97% | 1.00 |
| 25% | 7.39% | 9.04% | 0.82 |
| 10% | 6.81% | 10.17% | 0.67 |
| 0.5% | 6.73% | 10.58% | 0.64 |

F550 and F500 are both more complex than AND under the external logical proxy, yet they can exploit the `x4` patterns not covered by the training set to form larger margins on the seen samples. The mean signed margins are 0.378 for AND, 0.404 for F500, and 0.444 for F550. Lower raw BCE therefore systematically favors sample-specific shortcuts.

## 3. Patching holes causally changes the static winner

Patching only one side eliminates the old shortcut but may expose a new `copy x2` interpretation; after simultaneously covering the four key off-diagonal negatives, AND rises from 40.2% in the hard-exact cohort to 49.3% in the lowest 10% and 54.9% in the lowest 1%. Across six random negative-example subsets, AND is enriched in five and declines in one, proving that the determining factor is the specific constraint coverage, not the sample count or label ratio.

## 4. The minimal balanced intervention reveals a two-stage static reordering

After replacing only one sample, the coverage of the four `(x1,x2)` patterns changes from `3/2/1/4` to `3/2/2/3`. Among the 76,477 hard-exact prior samples:

- AND: 16.65% -> 28.10% at the lowest 0.2%;
- mean unseen exceptions: 1.733 -> 1.242;
- function entropy: 4.331 -> 3.303 bit.

However, the AND/F050 odds first fall from 1.508 to 1.388 at the lowest 10%, then rise to 2.231 at the lowest 0.2%. The static section itself already exhibits a staged reordering in which "intermediate loss first strengthens the shortcut, and deeper loss then strengthens AND".

## 5. Stage verdict

E12 simultaneously establishes:

1. lower raw BCE generally strongly enriches reusable simple rules;
2. within a fixed hard-compatible set, this tendency is not an unconditional law;
3. specific logical gaps in the training set can let a structurally more complex but margin-wise superior shortcut dominate over some loss interval;
4. changing only one discriminative sample can reshape the complete static function distribution;
5. the dominant function and the complexity ordering should be regarded as loss-resolved profiles, not fixed scalars.

This is not a failure of the core theory; rather, it corrects "simple function" from a fixed label valid across the whole range into something for which the full loss scale must be observed. The later Neural K-profile and the deep-tail crossing are precisely the formalization of this.

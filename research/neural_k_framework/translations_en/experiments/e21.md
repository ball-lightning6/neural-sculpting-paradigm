## Experiment overview {#overview}

## Purpose

E21 turns agreement from a passive statistic into a training-set intervention tool: it repeatedly queries the unseen input on which the current committee disagrees most, tries both labels separately, and chooses the branch that makes the function distribution contract faster or slower. It then uses a long anti-consensus prefix to exclude the early simple attractors layer by layer, before switching to pro-consensus so that the distribution re-condenses, and measures how the symbolic complexity of the stable endpoint changes.

## Why this experiment was run, and how it was judged {#motivation}

## 1. What agreement can tell us

For a fixed training protocol, high agreement of multiple seeds on unseen inputs indicates that the empirical function distribution is concentrated; it does not rely on the researcher knowing the teacher, so it can be used on data of unknown meaning. Low agreement only shows that extension competition remains; on its own it cannot distinguish insufficient data, label noise, optimization inaccessibility, or a multimodal landscape.

High pointwise agreement still does not guarantee that the full function has settled, so the experiment also reports:

- unseen pairwise agreement;
- vote entropy;
- exact modal full-function mass;
- 0.5% Hamming ball mass;
- fresh-seed fit rate.

## 2. Three greedy branches

Starting from a random balanced n=8 training set, each round selects the unseen input on which the current committee's prediction is closest to 50:50; this shares the basic idea of "using version-space disagreement to select queries" with [Query by Committee by Seung, Opper, and Sompolinsky (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1145/130385.130417). For labels 0/1, strictly paired initializations are trained from scratch separately:

- anti-consensus: choose the branch with lower final global agreement;
- pro-consensus: choose the branch with higher agreement;
- random-label: choose randomly among the fittable branches.

Label selection does not read any symbolic complexity. The formal pilot uses 8-bit, 16 starting points, 64-seed discovery, and 512 fresh-seed audits, extending to n=24.

## 3. Complexity frontier

Sixteen anti trunks are shared, with frozen snapshots at `n=8,16,24,32,48,64`; each snapshot switches to pro completion, adding at most 32 samples until the discovery stage reaches a narrow posterior, and is then audited with 512 fresh seeds. Only fresh-narrow endpoints enter the symbolic complexity analysis.

The pre-registered proxies include essential variables, ANF, decision tree, ROBDD, and linear threshold audits. Quadratic polynomial threshold is a post-hoc audit added after seeing the unexplained candidates and must be marked separately.

## 4. Limits

- pro/anti are greedy at each step and do not guarantee a globally optimal data sequence;
- high agreement does not equal external correctness;
- the pointwise majority function of a wide function distribution cannot be used for a stable program-complexity verdict;
- "readable" means that a short, extractable symbolic program exists; it does not mean a human can spot it at a glance;
- the current conclusions are limited to 8-bit tanh MLPs.

## Results, interpretation, and limits {#results}

## 1. Agreement can be causally controlled by label selection

Fresh-seed endpoints of the 16 8-bit starting points:

| strategy | unseen agreement | vote entropy | exact modal median | 0.5% ball mass |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| anti | 0.96977 | 0.06913 | 0.01563 | 0.04883 |
| pro | 0.99904 | 0.00259 | 0.99902 | 1.00000 |
| random | 0.97599 | 0.05575 | 0.04590 | 0.15625 |

anti is below pro in 16/16, fair sign probability `1.53e-5`; anti is below random in 12/16. Under the narrow-posterior threshold: anti 0/16, pro 15/16, random 0/16. Anti does not make agreement drop absolutely every round; rather, when both labels could contract, it chooses the one that contracts more slowly.

Of the 15 stable pro functions, post-run LP audit found 15/15 to be general linear thresholds, of which 7 are equal-weight signed thresholds and 8 are small-integer weighted thresholds. The only path that failed the linear audit is also exactly the only one that did not reach a narrow posterior, so it cannot serve as a stable complex counterexample.

## 2. Longer anti prefixes raise the cost of re-condensation

| anti cutoff | fresh narrow/16 | median extra pro samples |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 16 | 6.5 |
| 16 | 16 | 5.5 |
| 24 | 13 | 8.5 |
| 32 | 13 | 10.0 |
| 48 | 11 | 17.0 |
| 64 | 11 | 15.0 |

Across the 80 fresh-narrow endpoints, the Spearman between cutoff and the number of extra pro samples is 0.433, `p=6e-5`.

## 3. Stable-endpoint complexity forms a clear frontier

From cutoff 8 to 64: mean essential variables rose from 5.06 to 7.91, median ANF terms from 24 to 100, and median ROBDD nodes from 10 to 29. The Spearmans with cutoff are about 0.786, 0.662, and 0.777 respectively, all highly significant; among the 11 jointly surviving paired starting points, all three measures increased in 11/11.

The proportion of general linear thresholds fell from 100% to 0%. Of the 31 candidates not explained by the pre-registered languages, the post-hoc quadratic PTF audit found 31/31 exactly representable; all 80 narrow endpoints are PTFs of degree no higher than 2, whereas 0/64 of the 64 random balanced functions belong to this family. The median number of quadratic support terms rose with cutoff from 6.5 to 31.

## 4. Verdict

E21 shows:

1. the training-set label path can systematically control the condensation speed of the function distribution;
2. maximally disagreed samples have high intervention value, but the label direction decides whether to contract or to maintain competition;
3. after excluding huge simple basins layer by layer, the samples needed for re-condensation and the endpoint program complexity rise together;
4. no high-consensus random lookup table was observed; instead, a complexity frontier from sparse linear to denser quadratic thresholds was obtained.

It does not prove that all high-consensus functions are human-readable; quadratic PTF is a post-hoc unifying finding. The more accurate empirical conclusion is: under the current protocol, the mappings that can induce stable full-function consensus with few samples remain highly structured, and the data budget needed for agreement convergence can serve as an operational cue of protocol-relative complexity.

## Experiment overview {#overview}

## Objective

Test whether, knowing only that the task is binary, that the class ratio is 5:5,
and one labeled anchor, the low-loss parameter volume can blindly select the
natural digit 0/1 partition among 126 candidate label assignments.

## Why this experiment was run, and how it was judged {#motivation}

## 1. Question

The 2017 random-label experiment showed that networks can fit both natural labels
and random labels. E26 does the reverse: it fixes ten unlabeled images and compares
the low-loss parameter volume of all feasible balanced label assignments under the
same architecture and reference measure.

Each panel contains 5 images of digit 0 and 5 images of digit 1. The first image is
fixed to label 0, the remaining labels are unknown, and candidates must keep the
5:5 ratio, so there are C(9,4)=126 partitions.

## 2. Preventing label leakage

SGD and SMC calibration use independent panels. The two formal evaluation panels
complete the writing of all candidate volumes to disk and compute their SHA256
while the true labels are still hidden; only afterwards is the natural partition
revealed and ranked.

## 3. Verdict

If the natural partition becomes top1 at the reliable shared loss levels of both
independent panels, and its normalized volume mass rises systematically as loss
decreases, this supports the claim that the network's static geometry prefers the
natural digit grouping.

Failure cannot be automatically attributed to the sampler; it could also indicate
that the current preprocessing or architecture prefers other partitions, such as
brightness or position. Success applies only to the protocol with a known binary
task, a 5:5 ratio, and one anchor; it does not amount to fully unsupervised
clustering.

## Results, interpretation, and limits {#results}

## 1. Frozen problem

Each formal panel contains 5 images of digit 0 and 5 images of digit 1. The first
image is fixed to label 0, the remaining labels are unknown, but candidate
partitions must keep the 5:5 ratio, so there are C(9,4)=126 candidates. The true
digit labels stay hidden until all static volumes have been written to disk and
their SHA256 computed.

The network is a 49->32->1 tanh MLP; the inputs are MNIST images fixedly
downsampled to 7x7 and mapped to [-1,1]; the parameter reference measure is the
standard Gaussian, and SMC mutation uses a pCN proposal that preserves this
measure. The calibration panel and the two formal evaluation panels do not
overlap.

## 2. Two blind-test panels

| loss threshold | panels where natural partition is top1 | median rank of natural partition | mean volume mass of natural partition | volume-weighted expected label accuracy |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.8 | 0/2 | 126 | 0.00723 | 0.5534 |
| 0.6 | 2/2 | 1 | 0.11858 | 0.7198 |
| 0.4 | 2/2 | 1 | 0.97427 | 0.9945 |
| 0.3 | 2/2 | 1 | 0.99972 | 0.99994 |

At shallow loss levels, the natural labeling even ranks last in both panels; as
loss decreases, it simultaneously overtakes all 125 balanced competing partitions
in both independent panels, and at the 0.3 level it occupies about 99.97% of the
normalized volume mass.

The natural candidate ID is 22 in panel 1 and 57 in panel 2. The SHA256 values of
the two unscored volume tables are:

- 7be87b1a3b7cd65e4ef742d8df19c63ad02baf538f0b6a938527d65a50daf467
- 7482f33cdd8cc40ace9b977725bd428a323fdc0486dd3f85c03070347fc7cba9

## 3. Conclusion

This experiment directly supports:

1. Once the number of classes, the 5:5 ratio, and one anchor are fixed, the
   low-loss static parameter volume of a finite-width MLP systematically prefers
   the natural 0/1 digit partition;
2. this preference is not a fixed prior ordering, but moves along the loss axis
   from last place to first place and eventually monopolizes the mass;
3. the low-loss volume can recover the natural classification without reading the
   nine hidden labels.

What cannot be claimed:

1. that this is fully unconstrained unsupervised learning;
2. that natural labels would still win when the class ratio is unknown;
3. that the result generalizes to other digits, architectures, preprocessing, or
   real clustering tasks;
4. that static volume computation is cheaper in cost than conventional supervised
   or clustering algorithms.

Within the release scope, this upload unit only documents the balanced experiment
with 5 zeros and 5 ones; the subsequent branch without the ratio constraint is
not included.

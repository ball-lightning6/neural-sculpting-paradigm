8-bit random memory: the full 80-item matrix

The near-linear relation between random-label memory complexity and sample count.

Scope and status

The user provided results_8bit_random_memory_scaling_w16_v1_package (1).zip. This package is the complete set of results re-run on the 5090, not the earlier 10-item stage package from the 4090 D.

  • 10 sample-size tiers: 32, 48, 64, 80, 96, 128, 160, 192, 224, 256.
  • 8 random datasets per tier, 80 items in total, all reaching mean BCE ≤ 0.001.
  • Each dataset index corresponds to an independent random label table and input permutation; the different n values sharing the same index use nested prefixes.
  • Network 8→16→16→1 tanh, 433 parameters; 0/1 inputs, standard Gaussian neural coordinates, both weights and biases scaled by fan-in.
  • Each item: 4 replicas × 2048 particles, 2 adaptation rounds and 12 fixed updates, float32 forward/particles, float64 log volumes, TF32 disabled.
  • Environment: RTX 5090, torch 2.8.0+cu128, Python 3.12.3. The sampling loop accumulated 17692.89 seconds, about 4.915 hours; startup and partial packaging time are counted separately.
  • This round was read-only auditing and offline statistics; no new training, SMC, automatic recheck, or failed-seed microanalysis was added.

Statistical conventions

Each dataset is first computed per replica: c=[K(eps_low)-K(eps_high)]/log2(eps_high/eps_low), then the replica median of that dataset is taken. The mean and standard deviation of the coefficients are then computed over the 8 independent datasets, without averaging the volumes first.

The main analysis keeps all 80 items, including those with warnings. Between-dataset SD and replica SD are reported separately. Replicas share the adaptive thresholds and step sizes, so their SD must not be treated as a strict confidence interval.

The linear relationship still holds, but not every dataset is equally clean

The fit over all 10 tier means is:

$$ c(n)\approx0.82354n+22.725,\qquad R^2=0.97238. $$

For the mean curves of the seven adjacent loss windows, R² ranges from about 0.9724 to 0.9871. The 8 independent dataset trajectories all increase tier by tier with n in the deepest window, with 72/72 adjacent increments positive.

The per-dataset trajectory R² values in the deepest window are approximately 0.9938, 0.9959, 0.9854, 0.8363, 0.9252, 0.9204, 0.9938, 0.9897 in order. So it is not the case that only dataset 1 shows approximate linearity; at the same time, one cannot claim that every dataset is close to a perfect straight line.

The between-dataset median curve serves as a robustness reference, fitted as c≈0.72409n+30.927, R²≈0.9960. This does not replace the mean-based main result, and even less does it mean removing high values.

Extra fluctuation at the two largest tiers

The 8 coefficients at n=256 are, in order, 218.15, 222.64, 208.38, 374.34, 306.35, 285.08, 216.31, 198.11. Datasets 4, 5, and 6 clearly pull the mean up; not every one of them triggered the same class of warning, so warning labels cannot be used to directly judge which are "spurious high values".

After observing this phenomenon, a range sensitivity analysis was done: fitting only the means with n≤192 gives c≈0.66645n+35.617, R²≈0.9978; its extrapolated predictions for 224 and 256 are about 13.4% and 23.0% below the actual means, respectively. This is a post-hoc diagnostic and is not dressed up as a pre-selected main interval.

The two largest tiers also show larger replica disagreement; for example, the replica SD of the coefficient for dataset 4 at n=224 is about 24.8%, and for dataset 6 at n=256 about 31.0%. 433 parameters is only mild overparameterization relative to 256 single-output samples. At present the contributions of genuine data difficulty, capacity pressure, and insufficient sampling mixing cannot be separated.

Reduced change in the tail

Across the 7 pre-specified adjacent windows, the local coefficients of all 80/80 curves decrease step by step.

  • From the 0.2→0.1 window to the 0.1→0.05 window, the median decrease of the coefficient is about 17.39%.
  • From the 0.005→0.002 window to the 0.002→0.001 window, the median decrease is about 3.79%.
  • The 10th to 90th percentile of the final decrease is 3.02% to 4.65%, with the full range 0.34% to 5.55%.
  • In 78/80 curves the final change does not exceed 5%, and in 80/80 it does not exceed 10%; there is no example where the final window rises instead.

This supports the slope gradually stabilizing over the finite low-loss region, but it does not prove the true epsilon→0 limit, nor does it directly measure the microscopic distribution of implementation complexity.

Independent verification

  • All 1200 snapshot files and 38400 groups of extracted parameters passed independent NumPy float64 forward recomputation.
  • The maximum logit difference is about 1.77e-5, and the maximum mean BCE difference is about 4.44e-8; no classification sign changes, no observed violations of the loss constraint.
  • Training labels at the final snapshots are all correct, and the reported fraction of fully fitted endpoint particles is 1 for all tasks.
  • Layer-wise and per-node K show no negative increments; the per-dataset window coefficients agree exactly with the in-package results, with the maximum difference in summary means about 5.68e-14.
  • The seeds, labels, and nesting order of the 8 complete random datasets were independently reconstructed and agree; the data hashes are all distinct; the Hamming distances between different label tables range from 105 to 143.
  • These checks confirm that the implementation and the constraints are consistent; they do not certify that the global SMC mass estimate is unbiased.

Cross-GPU consistency reference

For the same dataset 1, the final-window coefficients differ between the 4090 D and the 5090 by about 0.14% to 6.23%, mostly small. At n=256 the value changes from 216.95 to 218.15, a change of about 0.55%; the change at n=192 is larger, about -6.23%.

The sampling time for this dataset changed from 3045.29 seconds to 2197.07 seconds, a speed factor of about 1.386. The data and seeds are identical, so it cannot be counted as a 9th independent random dataset, nor treated as a retest on an independent random stream.

Why this calibrates the complexity definition

Random labels offer no reusable target rule; adding independent examples generally adds constraints that must be memorized separately. Here the sample count n is an external memory burden fixed before measuring Neural K. It is not a "complexity" defined after the fact from neural volume. We can therefore use it to test whether negative log parameter mass and its loss-resolved contraction respond to a nonreusable implementation cost.

This experiment measures the finite-window coefficient c(n)=[K(0.001)-K(0.002)]/log2(2), not the absolute Kolmogorov complexity of each complete function. The mean curve over ten sample sizes and eight independent datasets is approximately linear (R²=0.97238); every adjacent increment in all eight deepest-window trajectories is positive. The correspondence between an independently controlled burden and the volume-contraction cost provides evidence of construct validity for interpreting the Neural K-profile as a neural-protocol-relative implementation complexity. It is more than a relabeling of volume.

This remains an indirect calibration, not a proof that the complexity definition is universally correct. Capacity pressure and SMC mixing at larger n have not been fully separated; other architectures, encodings, or losses may give different coefficients; and random-memory burden is not machine-independent Kolmogorov complexity. The supported claim is an approximately linear response in this protocol and this finite loss window.

Conclusions that can be supported

Compared with the single-dataset pilot sampling, there is now fuller support for the statement that "at finite loss, as the random memory burden increases, the volume contraction coefficient shows a reproducible approximately linear response". The reduced change at the low-loss end is also reproduced across all 80 curves.

Not yet supported: strict proportionality over the full range, exact linearity for every dataset, architecture-independent coefficients, equivalence to Kolmogorov complexity, or that all deepest tails at the largest n are already well mixed. The upturn at large n is not directly asserted to be a superlinear law, nor is it removed as sampling error.

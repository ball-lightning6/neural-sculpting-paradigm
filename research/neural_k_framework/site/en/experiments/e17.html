E17: Static Deep-Loss Mass versus Optimizer Transport

Measure how closely static constrained-SMC function distributions predict AdamW/SGD ensembles and identify conditions where optimizer history dominates.

Experiment overview

Purpose

E17 uses a brute-force prior, rare-event constrained SMC, three optimizer crossings, 32,768-seed AdamW, continued training from SMC deep-tail starting points, and a seed/time mixing experiment to systematically test how strongly the static loss volume predicts real training, and whether the optimizer is merely an unbiased or second-order sampling correction of that static distribution.

Why this experiment was run, and how it was judged

1. The two-layer volume hypothesis

At the time, the following distinction was drawn:

  1. Static layer: whether lower loss changes function mass and biases it toward economical implementations;
  2. Training layer: whether real generalization is dominated by this static mass, leaving the optimizer with only second-order influence.

E17 specifically stress-tests the second layer, while calibrating the rare-event measurements of the first layer.

2. Experiment chain

A. Brute-force prior and optimizer crossing

4-bit balanced AND, 4 -> 16 x 2 -> 1 tanh; 33,554,432 untrained networks and 4,096 paired initial weights, comparing the function distributions of AdamW, full-batch SGD, and momentum SGD when they first cross the same raw BCE.

B. Constrained SMC

32,768 particles, 8 replicas, driving BCE down layer by layer through prior-preserving MCMC; cross-calibrated against the brute-force prior at 0.68/0.65/0.60, then pushed into the hard-exact deep tail below 0.07.

C. Real training from the deep tail

  • Real training of 32,768-seed AdamW on the same network and the same training set;
  • 4,096 starting points drawn from the SMC deep-tail D440 weights, pairing normal AdamW with AdamW projected back into the initialization box at every step, to test whether the static deep tail flows back to AND.

D. Time mixing

8-bit AND and random labels, 2,048 seeds; comparing 64 late checkpoints from a single trajectory, an IID-64 seed sample, and a pool of 32 trajectories, to judge whether time sampling can substitute for the multi-seed distribution.

3. Preregistered boundaries

  • Static mass and optimizer crossings must be compared at the same loss and with sufficient prior/SMC support;
  • Agreement of the top functions does not equal identity of the full distribution;
  • SMC measures a specified parameter measure, not a coordinate-independent volume;
  • The same hard ID may still lie in different continuous flow tubes;
  • Similarity of a single agreement statistic cannot prove mixing of the full distribution.

Results, interpretation, and limits

1. Shallow-layer static mass has strong first-order predictive power

At the three BCE layers 0.68/0.65/0.60 where the brute-force prior is reliable, the static distribution and all three optimizers always share constant zero as their top function. The fraction of optimizer mass covered by the static top-100 functions deepens with loss, reaching:

BCE Adam SGD Momentum
0.68 66.4% 74.5% 77.4%
0.65 88.7% 95.7% 97.1%
0.60 98.7% 100% 100%

But all JSD/TV deviations exceed finite-sample noise; the optimizer is not an unbiased static sampler.

2. SMC is calibrated in the brute-force overlap region and reveals deep-tail reordering

SMC reproduces the brute-force volume fractions and the 65,536-dimensional function distribution at 0.68/0.65/0.60, with JSD of about 0.00677/0.00297/0.00144 respectively. The dominant static state of the deep tail passes successively through:

high-loss constant zero
-> 0x5000 shortcut
-> D000/D040
-> F040/D440

All particles are already hard-exact below 0.20, yet continuing to lower the soft BCE still strongly reorders the unseen continuation. At 0.07, D440 is 50.99%, F040 27.20%, D040 16.35%, and AND is 0%. The static landscape itself contains rich stage structure, but it is not ordered by the length of the human AND formula.

3. There is a loss-dependent lag between the optimizer and the static geometry

In the 0.50 static shell, 0x5000 is 42.33% and Adam only 13.62%, while SGD/Momentum still put 83.42%/97.80% on constant zero; by 0.40, the 0x5000 share of SGD/Momentum rises to 78.39%/69.12%, approaching the static 70.04% again. The relationship is not a fixed "which optimizer is closest to equilibrium"; rather, the static reordering happens first and the different optimizers follow with their own time lags.

4. Deep-tail AdamW can completely overturn the static winner

On the same training set, 32,768-seed AdamW at step 11,444 yields AND 76.52% and F040 23.48%; static SMC at BCE no higher than 0.065 gives AND 0% and D440 55.70%. A same-loss comparison at about BCE 0.10 already has JSD 0.301 and TV 0.438; the final distributions have JSD 0.760 and TV 0.765.

Therefore optimizer reweighting is not always a mild second-order term; it is enough to dominate the final function selection.

5. Starting from the SMC deep tail shows that path history is irreducible

Continuing training from 4,096 real weight points drawn from D440: normal AdamW lowers the loss by about four million times, ending at D040 51.56%, F040 48.44%, AND 0%; the projected group stays at D440 100%. F040 on the random-initialization path migrates to AND, but the same hard ID on the SMC path migrates to D040. The same hard function, training set, and optimizer are still not enough to predict the future; the continuous state inside the cell and the entry history do not commute.

6. Single-trajectory time sampling is not equal to the multi-seed distribution

For 8-bit AND, 64 late checkpoints of a single chain typically visit only 5--6 functions, while IID-64 covers about 28--60; with random labels the single chain visits 14 and IID visits 64. Single-chain agreement is about 0.995, while the seed reference can be as low as 0.951 or 0.800. A pool of 32 trajectories can approximate some low-order agreement statistics, but the full fingerprint and covariance are still not equivalent.

7. Final verdict

E17 rules out two extremes:

  • Static volume is not an irrelevant backdrop; it strongly constrains the candidate family and the reordering structure;
  • The optimizer is also by no means a transparent or necessarily second-order sampler; it can produce huge, history-dependent reweighting.

The empirical decomposition can be written as:

$$ Q_O(f\mid D,L)\propto V_\mu(f;D,L)R_O(f;D,L), $$

But this is only layered bookkeeping, not a known multiplicative law. R_O contains entry, flow tube, velocity, and dwell history; its microscopic theory is outside the scope of this paper. Multiple seeds form an optimizer-induced ensemble; single-trajectory checkpoints cannot masquerade as independent Bayesian posterior samples.

E26: Balanced Unlabeled MNIST Label Volume

Test whether low-loss parameter volume can blindly recover the natural MNIST 0/1 grouping among all 126 balanced labelings.

Scripts
experiment_mnist_unlabeled_label_volume.py

Experiment overview

Objective

Test whether, knowing only that the task is binary, that the class ratio is 5:5, and one labeled anchor, the low-loss parameter volume can blindly select the natural digit 0/1 partition among 126 candidate label assignments.

Entry points

Upload boundary

This unit only includes the balanced-candidate experiment with 5 zeros and 5 ones. The follow-up 512-candidate experiment that removes the class-ratio constraint, and its deep-tail follow-up, are not part of this upload.

Why this experiment was run, and how it was judged

1. Question

The 2017 random-label experiment showed that networks can fit both natural labels and random labels. E26 does the reverse: it fixes ten unlabeled images and compares the low-loss parameter volume of all feasible balanced label assignments under the same architecture and reference measure.

Each panel contains 5 images of digit 0 and 5 images of digit 1. The first image is fixed to label 0, the remaining labels are unknown, and candidates must keep the 5:5 ratio, so there are C(9,4)=126 partitions.

2. Preventing label leakage

SGD and SMC calibration use independent panels. The two formal evaluation panels complete the writing of all candidate volumes to disk and compute their SHA256 while the true labels are still hidden; only afterwards is the natural partition revealed and ranked.

3. Verdict

If the natural partition becomes top1 at the reliable shared loss levels of both independent panels, and its normalized volume mass rises systematically as loss decreases, this supports the claim that the network's static geometry prefers the natural digit grouping.

Failure cannot be automatically attributed to the sampler; it could also indicate that the current preprocessing or architecture prefers other partitions, such as brightness or position. Success applies only to the protocol with a known binary task, a 5:5 ratio, and one anchor; it does not amount to fully unsupervised clustering.

Results, interpretation, and limits

1. Frozen problem

Each formal panel contains 5 images of digit 0 and 5 images of digit 1. The first image is fixed to label 0, the remaining labels are unknown, but candidate partitions must keep the 5:5 ratio, so there are C(9,4)=126 candidates. The true digit labels stay hidden until all static volumes have been written to disk and their SHA256 computed.

The network is a 49->32->1 tanh MLP; the inputs are MNIST images fixedly downsampled to 7x7 and mapped to [-1,1]; the parameter reference measure is the standard Gaussian, and SMC mutation uses a pCN proposal that preserves this measure. The calibration panel and the two formal evaluation panels do not overlap.

At shallow loss levels, the natural labeling even ranks last in both panels; as loss decreases, it simultaneously overtakes all 125 balanced competing partitions in both independent panels, and at the 0.3 level it occupies about 99.97% of the normalized volume mass.

3. Conclusion

This experiment directly supports:

  1. Once the number of classes, the 5:5 ratio, and one anchor are fixed, the low-loss static parameter volume of a finite-width MLP systematically prefers the natural 0/1 digit partition;
  2. this preference is not a fixed prior ordering, but moves along the loss axis from last place to first place and eventually monopolizes the mass;
  3. the low-loss volume can recover the natural classification without reading the nine hidden labels.

What cannot be claimed:

  1. that this is fully unconstrained unsupervised learning;
  2. that natural labels would still win when the class ratio is unknown;
  3. that the result generalizes to other digits, architectures, preprocessing, or real clustering tasks;
  4. that static volume computation is cheaper in cost than conventional supervised or clustering algorithms.

Within the release scope, this upload unit only documents the balanced experiment with 5 zeros and 5 ones; the subsequent branch without the ratio constraint is not included.

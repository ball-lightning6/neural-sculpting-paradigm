E30: Weight Decay Reshapes the Static Complete-Function Landscape

Clarify that grokking does not require weight decay and that explicit L2 helps by reshaping the same static function landscape rather than introducing a special mechanism.

Scripts
experiment_weight_decay_static_landscape.py

Experiment overview

Purpose

Show that grokking does not require weight decay; explicit L2 helps grokking because it changes the static loss landscape over which the same set of functions competes, not because it introduces a mysterious or special learning mechanism.

Why this experiment was run, and how it was judged

1. Question

The original grokking experiments by Power et al. showed that weight decay markedly promotes delayed generalization, but grokking and rule recovery do not take weight decay as a necessary condition. Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2025) also found high-norm generalizing solutions with WanD without explicit regularization. A common explanation still treats weight decay as a special intervention in the optimizer dynamics; but for explicit L2, the optimizer in fact only receives the gradient of the full scalar objective

$$ J_\lambda(\theta) = L_D(\theta)+\lambda\lVert\theta\rVert^2/2 $$

The two most basic objects of the original framework are precisely the loss landscape and the optimizer; explicit L2 merely adds a term to the same scalar loss and is not a new theoretical mechanism. The preferred prediction should be: the raw-loss landscape without L2 can already support rule takeover, and adding L2 strengthens this preference by changing the static landscape.

2. Competing explanations

  1. Full static-landscape explanation. At similar raw BCE, increasing $\lambda$ increases the static mass of correct AND continuations and compresses the function diversity of incorrect continuations.
  2. Fit-depth-only explanation. The L2 conditions have more AND only because their raw BCE is lower; after matched-BCE the difference should vanish.
  3. optimizer-specific explanation. The static landscape is nearly unchanged, and real training differences come mainly from the path.

E30 directly adjudicates only the first two. The third is worth testing only after reliable static measurements leave a clear residual.

3. Fixed task and measure

  • Input: all 8-bit strings; the target is x0 AND x1; the remaining 6 bits are nuisance.
  • Training set: 10 samples from each of the four main semantic cells, 40 frozen samples in total.
  • Network: 8->16->16->1 tanh, 433 standardized Gaussian coordinates.
  • Reference measure: coordinate-wise independent $N(0,1)$; the network forward pass uses fan-in scaling.
  • Static sampling: direct constrained SMC; 16 replicas, 8192 particles each.
  • Function audit: 2048 parameters saved per replica, with the hard function decided on all 256 points.

4. Staged threshold freezing

This experiment is not a fully blind single-step preregistration, but a staged calibration based only on BCE, norms, and sampling diagnostics. Thresholds must not be adjusted based on the AND mass to be reported.

The final three main conditions are:

$\lambda$ Static constraint Actual raw BCE mean
0 $L_D\le0.00268$ 0.00258150
$5\times10^{-5}$ $J_\lambda\le0.0160$ 0.00251046
$10^{-4}$ $J_\lambda\le0.0211$ 0.00258978

The middle point's BCE is about 3% lower than the two endpoints, so the conditions can only be called approximately matched-BCE. All figures and tables must also show the actual BCE; the three points must not be written as exactly equal.

5. Quality gates

  • replica log-volume standard deviation no higher than 1 nat;
  • maximum probe cross-replica standard deviation no higher than 0.03;
  • replica standard deviation of the full AND mass no higher than 0.05;
  • all saved samples satisfy the constraint and 100% hard-fit the training set;
  • use the replica, not the particle, as the paired statistical unit.

Floating-point boundary checks use a relative tolerance plus 16 float32 ULPs; the minimum tolerance for deep losses remains $10^{-12}$.

6. External connection

Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2025) used the Wang--Landau method to observe a high-entropy advantage of low-loss generalizing states in a modular-arithmetic Transformer. E30 does not reproduce their full Transformer entropy map; instead, in a rigorously auditable 433-parameter system, it directly compares the function-level static mass of the explicit-L2 full objective.

Results, interpretation, and limits

1. Three-point main result

The raw BCE of the three conditions differs by at most about 3.1%, and all saved networks 100% hard-fit the training set:

$\lambda$ raw BCE mean full AND mass replica range number of full functions half norm mean
0 0.00258150 53.683% 50.391%--57.178% 4190 369.70
$5\times10^{-5}$ 0.00251046 58.423% 50.830%--66.406% 1246 268.76
$10^{-4}$ 0.00258978 73.318% 66.016%--78.125% 86 184.97

The full AND mass increases monotonically with the L2 coefficient, while the full-function diversity drops from 4190 to 1246 and then to 86.

The middle point's raw BCE is slightly lower, which may slightly inflate its AND mass; but the BCE of $\lambda=0$ and $10^{-4}$ differ by only 0.32%, so the main endpoint difference is not affected by this boundary.

4. Conceptual verdict

With training fit depth approximately matched, explicit L2 still systematically increases the static mass of correct AND continuations and compresses the full-function diversity. The current simplest explanation is therefore:

weight decay reshuffles the parameter mass of training-set-consistent continuations by changing the low-$J$ geometry of the full scalar objective; its effect on this round of grokking-style function selection does not need an optimizer-specific mechanism as a backstop.

The no-WD condition by itself already gives AND about 53.7% static mass, showing that the rule can become the dominant candidate in a pure raw-loss landscape; weight decay is not a necessary condition for grokking. Its role in this experiment is to further strengthen an already existing static preference, not to create a new mechanism unrelated to the original framework.

This result does not add a new theoretical element; it clarifies the direct use of the original framework: the most basic objects remain the loss landscape and the optimizer. Any term genuinely written into the scalar loss, whether called a regularizer, an auxiliary loss, or anything else, merely co-shapes the same landscape and has no special theoretical status. When analyzing such interventions, first measure the corresponding static function landscape; only if a residual remains should the optimizer be discussed.

5. Why this is not "AND already had a lower total norm"

In the fixed cross-section with no WD and BCE<=0.01, the ranking of total L2 norm between AND and other continuations is nearly random: the loss-stratified low-norm AUC median is 0.5088. The role of L2 is not to read a pre-existing obvious norm label, but to let BCE+lambda*R jointly define different BCE--norm Pareto subsets.

6. Deeper-layer and stronger-coefficient boundaries

  • At the deeper layer with $\lambda=10^{-4}$ and $J\le0.0186$, all low-$J$ full functions found by the 16 far-apart replicas are AND; but the absolute logV standard deviation is 5.66 nat and the genealogies are severely degenerate, so we only write "all observed modes are AND" and do not certify an exact 100%.
  • For $\lambda=2\times10^{-4}$ at $J\le0.0396$, BCE is 0.00535 and AND is 73.42%; going further down to $J\le0.03545$, BCE is 0.00470 and AND is 90.79%, but the logV standard deviation rises to 1.48 nat. Neither point is a matched-BCE main dose, so neither enters the main curve.

7. What can and cannot be claimed

What can be claimed:

  1. Under this task, architecture, Gaussian measure, and approximately matched-BCE protocol, increasing the explicit L2 coefficient increases the static full-function mass of AND;
  2. the raw-loss landscape without weight decay already supports rule dominance by itself, and weight decay is not a necessary condition for grokking;
  3. the enhancement brought by weight decay can be explained by the change in the static landscape, with no extra assumption that the optimizer recognizes the regularizer; other terms written into the loss are the same and are not special.

What cannot be claimed:

  1. that the same mechanism has already been directly demonstrated on the original Mod97 Transformer;
  2. that decoupled AdamW is strictly equal to the explicit-L2 scalar objective;
  3. that the AND mass is monotonic in arbitrary $\lambda$, or that $\lambda=2\times10^{-4}$ has completed matched-BCE;
  4. that the deep-layer static AND probability has been strictly certified as 100%.

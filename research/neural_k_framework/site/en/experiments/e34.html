Experiment increment · E34

E34 Conditional rule duel

Full protocol, results, and boundaries for E34 Conditional rule duel.

Status: both the high-gap pilot and the full round robin are complete. Evidence level: a priori prediction of a preregistered complexity order, under a single 8→16→1 tanh protocol.

1. Question and Design

Can the independently measured 4-bit single-rule Neural K ordering predict, in advance, which rule real Adam training will select under direct competition? (Together with E36's 48-pair panel, this experiment belongs to the "rule duel" family; it is the first one run in that family and the one with the strongest signal.)

The 8-bit input is split into a front and a back 4-bit block; rule f reads the front block and rule g reads the back block:

  • Training set: all 128 inputs with f(front) == g(back), labeled with the common output;
  • Conflict set: the 128 inputs with f(front) != g(back), with no external ground truth assigned; only the competition preference is tested;
  • Each pair is run with front/back positions swapped and is seed-matched; 8→16→1 tanh (161 parameters), full-batch Adam (lr=0.01, wd=0); matched-loss thresholds 0.1/0.03/0.01/0.003/0.001.

The rule panel and the expected ordering were frozen from the independently measured 4-bit Neural K curves before any training results were read: parity1 < majority3 < MUX3 < parity2 < parity3 < parity4.

2. Results

High-gap pilot: 5 duels × both directions × 256 seeds; the low-K rule was selected in 100% of all runs.

Full round robin (all 15 unordered pairs of the 6 rules × both directions × 5 loss cross-sections = 75 verdicts): 75/75 all conform to the pre-frozen complexity total order. Per-pair results at the main cross-section (BCE=0.001) are in FULL_TOURNAMENT_RESULTS.md; representative non-saturated readings:

  • parity1 vs majority3: low-K match rate 0.761 (the remainder are hybrid functions);
  • MUX3 vs parity2: 0.606 (seed-matched win rate 0.617, Wilson [0.556, 0.675]) — this is the only non-overwhelming verdict among the 15 pairs; the direction still conforms to the frozen total order, but the preference decreases monotonically from 0.684 to 0.606 as the loss deepens (no reversal; retained as an open reading).

3. Boundaries That Must Be Retained Together with the Results

  1. The conflict set has no external ground truth; what is measured is which complete extension the optimizer more often follows when the training set cannot distinguish the two rules — not "which rule is correct";
  2. In most verdicts the networks form hybrid functions (e.g., in parity1 vs majority3 the low-K rule wins on all 256 matched seeds, yet the average match rate is only 0.761) — "preference for the low-K rule" refers to a function population preference and does not require every network to replicate the full rule;
  3. A single network and data protocol; we do not claim that the win rate equals a softmax of the K gap, nor do we claim pairwise monotonicity;
  4. Relationship to E36 (the 48-pair panel): E36 shows an average preference close to 0.5 in the small-K-gap band (20–40 bits), indicating that the strong signal of this experiment depends on a sufficiently large complexity gap; the complete picture comes only from reading the two together.

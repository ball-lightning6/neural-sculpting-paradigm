All 16 inputs in a four-bit truth table can be classified correctly even when some outputs barely have the right sign. How many parameter settings still qualify if we lower the ceiling on mean binary cross-entropy from 0.01 to 0.001? Here, "how many" means the probability of drawing such a setting from a specified distribution, the reference measure. Each halving of that probability adds one bit to the cost K. Earlier Gaussian measurements showed K rising almost linearly with log precision. That curve alone could not tell us how much its shape depended on the weight given to different radii.

Section 1.3 reweights the same sampled parameters by distance from the origin, keeping the distribution of directions and the mean squared radius fixed. For four-bit parity, the fitted Gaussian line rises 8.85 bits per unit on the log precision scale `a` used there. A heavier tail (p = 1.5) gives 8.28; a lighter one (p = 3) gives 9.62. In all three cases the last segment is only about 2% to 3% less steep than the first.

The nearly straight shape persists. Its slope measures effective complexity for that measure and window, not a universal constant as loss approaches zero. Section 4 separates the contributions from different radii to see how they can shrink at different rates yet yield a nearly straight curve together.
